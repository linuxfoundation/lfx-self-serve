Newsletters let a foundation or project send an email update to the people in a group, then see who opened it and which links they clicked. A newsletter is not a mailing list: it goes to the members of one group whose type is **Newsletter**, and it is written and sent from the **Newsletters** page, not from **Mailing Lists**.

## Who can use Newsletters

| What                                                                       | Who                                                                      |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Read the newsletters you receive (**Me** lens)                             | Every signed-in user                                                     |
| Write, send, and schedule newsletters (**Foundation** or **Project** lens) | Executive directors, and people who can manage the foundation or project |

If you don't see **Newsletters** in the sidebar of the **Foundation** or **Project** lens, you don't have permission to send newsletters for it. Contact the foundation or project's executive director.

## Before you start: create a Newsletter group

A newsletter is sent to the members of a group whose type is **Newsletter**. Only groups of that type can be picked as a newsletter's audience; other groups, such as a Technical Steering Committee or a Working Group, are not listed.

To create one:

1. Switch to your **Foundation** or **Project** lens.
2. Select **Groups**, then **Create Group**.
3. On the first step, choose **Newsletter** as the group type.
4. Finish the remaining steps, and add the people who should receive the newsletter as members.

See [Create a group](../../committees/create-committee/) for every step. You can also start from the newsletter itself: if the foundation or project has no Newsletter group yet, the **Audience** step offers **Create newsletter group**, which opens group creation in a new tab with the Newsletter type already chosen. When you're done, come back and select **Refresh groups**.

## Write and send a newsletter

1. Switch to your **Foundation** or **Project** lens and select **Newsletters** in the sidebar.
2. Select **Create Newsletter**.
3. Work through the three steps: **Audience**, **Content**, and **Send**. Use **Next** and **Previous** to move between them, and **Preview** to see the newsletter as recipients will.

Once you have picked an audience and entered both a subject and a body, your draft saves automatically as you work. You can also select **Save as Draft** and come back to it later from the **Drafts** tab. Until all three are filled in, nothing is saved, so if you leave the page earlier your work is lost.

### Audience

Under **Group**, pick the Newsletter group to send to. The recipient count opens a **Recipients** list so you can check who will receive it.

If someone is missing, enter their email under **Someone missing? Add them to this group** and select **Add**. They are added to the group, so they also receive future newsletters sent to it.

If no Newsletter group exists, the step says "There are no groups in _name_ yet" or "None of the groups in _name_ are newsletter groups". Create a Newsletter group as described above, or ask a manager of the foundation or project to create one, then select **Refresh groups**.

### Content

- **Subject**: the headline recipients see in their inbox.
- **Body**: the newsletter itself. You can paste directly from Google Docs or Word. Markdown isn't supported.

To have AI draft the newsletter, select **Generate this with AI**, paste your raw notes into **Your raw content**, and select **Generate**. Generating replaces anything already in the subject and body, so you're asked to confirm first if they aren't empty. Review and edit the result before you send it.

### Send

The **Send** step summarizes the audience size, who the newsletter is from, where replies go, and the subject.

- Select **Send test** to send a test copy to your own email address only.
- Choose **Send immediately** and select **Send now**, or choose **Schedule for later**, pick a time, and select **Schedule**. A scheduled time must be at least 35 minutes from now and no more than 72 hours ahead.

A scheduled newsletter can't be edited. To change it, find it on the **Scheduled** tab and select **Cancel schedule** on its row, then open it from the **Drafts** tab to edit it. It goes back to being a draft and the time you picked is kept so you can schedule it again. You can't cancel when it is too close to the send time: you see **Too late to cancel**, and the newsletter goes out as scheduled.

## Personalization

Newsletters can't be personalized for each recipient. There are no merge fields or placeholders such as `{{first_name}}`: every recipient gets the same subject and body. Write a greeting that addresses the whole community instead, for example "Hello, community".

## Manage your newsletters

The **Newsletters** page has four tabs:

- **Drafts**: newsletters you haven't sent yet. Select one to keep editing, or **Delete** it.
- **Scheduled**: newsletters waiting to go out, with the time they will be sent. **Cancel schedule** turns one back into a draft, unless it is too close to the send time.
- **Sent**: newsletters that have gone out, with the number of recipients and the open rate.
- **Opt-out**: people who unsubscribed from your newsletters, and when.

### See how a newsletter performed

Select a newsletter on the **Sent** tab to open its analytics:

- **Recipients** (delivered and failed), **Unique opens**, **Total opens**, and **Open rate**. When there is click data, also **Unique clicks**, **Total clicks**, **Click rate**, and **Click-to-open rate**.
- **Page views**: how many people opened the newsletter's page in LFX Self Serve. Each signed-in reader counts once, however many times they come back.
- **Total views**: **Total opens** plus **Page views**. This is the view count readers see on the newsletter.
- **Shares**: how many times readers shared the newsletter with the **Share** button in LFX Self Serve, split by where they shared it (X, LinkedIn, Reddit, Email, or Copy link). Each person counts once per destination. Shares from the links inside the email itself aren't counted.
- **Opens over time**, **Clicks over time**, and **Top clicked links**.
- **Recipient engagement**: each recipient's delivery status, opens, and clicks. Filter by **Opened**, **Clicked**, **Not opened**, or **Failed**.

Select the failed count to open **Failed deliveries**, which lists the addresses that didn't receive the newsletter. **Copy all** copies them so you can check them.

## Read the newsletters you receive

In the **Me** lens, select **Newsletters** in the sidebar. You see one card for each foundation where you are a member of a Newsletter group. The newsletters shown follow your group membership, so when you join a Newsletter group you can also read the newsletters it sent before you joined. A foundation whose Newsletter group hasn't sent anything yet still gets a card. Select **View newsletters** to see that foundation's newsletters, search them, and filter by project. Select a newsletter to open it on its own page. To go back, use the breadcrumb at the top of the page: select the foundation's name to return to its newsletter list, or **Newsletters** to return to the foundation cards.

When you open a newsletter, two counts sit to the left of it (above it on a small screen): the eye icon shows how many times the newsletter has been viewed. Views add up two things: every email open (one person opening the email twice counts twice) and each person who reads the newsletter on its page in LFX Self Serve (counted once per person, however often they return). Someone who opens the email and then reads it online counts in both. The paper-plane icon is the share button, with the number of times the newsletter has been shared below it. To share a newsletter, select the paper-plane icon, then pick X, LinkedIn, Reddit, or Email, or select **Copy link**. The **Views** and **Shares** columns on a foundation's newsletter list show the same counts. Each person counts once per destination, so sharing to LinkedIn twice counts once. If the view count isn't available, the eye icon is hidden; the share count appears once the newsletter has been shared.

If you aren't a member of any Newsletter group, the page says "No newsletters yet". Join a foundation or project's Newsletter group to see its newsletters.

From a newsletter or a foundation card you can select **Invite a colleague** or **Invite** to invite a colleague to the foundation's newsletter. To send an invitation, you must be a member of the foundation's newsletter group and have a verified email at one of the member organizations named in the invite dialog. Your colleague's company email must also be at one of those member organizations.

### Reading without an LFX account

Newsletters are a member benefit, so an employee of a member company can read them without signing in:

- **From an invitation email.** Your colleague selects **Accept invitation**. If they aren't signed in, a window over the page shows the foundation's logo and their email address, which they can't change. Only the first few characters of the address are shown (for example `ja***@acme***.com`), in case the link was forwarded. They select **Continue**, their email is added to the newsletter's list, and the latest newsletter opens. The invitation link works for 30 days. If they're already signed in, they go to the foundation's newsletters and confirm there, as before.
- **From a shared link.** Someone who opens a link to a newsletter without signing in is asked for their work email. The window explains what happens next: if the email is at a member company, **Continue** opens that newsletter and LFX emails them a confirmation link. Their email is added to the newsletter's list, so future issues arrive automatically, only after they open that link. A personal address, or an address at a company that isn't a member, can't open the newsletter.

Either way, a blurred preview of the newsletter (its subject, first image, and opening lines) shows behind the window, and the full newsletter appears only after **Continue**. The window also offers **Sign in** for people who have an LFX account. Under **Continue**, a line notes that the site uses cookies and that continuing means agreeing to the use of their email under the Linux Foundation **Privacy Policy** and **Terms of Use**, with a **Cookie settings** link; where no cookies are used for tracking until a visitor agrees (or they've already made a choice), the usual cookie banner isn't shown on these pages. Elsewhere the usual banner still appears. The same links appear at the bottom of the page. Reading without signing in lasts until the browser is closed or for 12 hours, whichever comes first, and the LFX sidebar isn't shown.

If you open a link to a newsletter you can't read, you see a message such as **Restricted Newsletter** or **This newsletter is a member benefit** instead of the newsletter.
