# Company Lens all employee view (access gate and redaction tools)

Internal engineering note for epic #5845. Not Help Center copy. User-facing text says "company", never "organization" (epic #5060); identifiers keep the `org` names.

The all employee view is a **mode** of the Company Lens (`OrgLensViewMode = 'full' | 'employee'`, `packages/shared`): a reduced, read-only, privacy-safe view for an ordinary employee of an LF member company. Counts, charts and company-level totals only; no names, avatars or email addresses unless a reviewed exception says so. No Project detail card panel opens, Technical or Ecosystem Influence alike, so no person's name shows on any page. A later update will reopen some of these panels under a different access model.

## Who is in it

`assertOrgLensRead` (`server/helpers/org-lens-read-access.helper.ts`) has a third qualification, `employee-view`, tried **last**: after the roster (`org-grant`) and the authorizer (`auditor-entitlement`) have both answered cleanly and refused. A grant holder or LF staff therefore never reaches it and never sees less.

`OrgEmployeeViewService` (`server/services/org-employee-view.service.ts`) proves both of these for the **effective** user (the impersonation target, never the admin). To see a company's all employee view, a user must have:

1. **A validated email on the domain of an active Linux Foundation member company.** A **verified** email (`resolveVerifiedUserEmails`, strict) on the `company_domain` of the company's active Linux Foundation membership. **Interim, decided by the product owner:** member-service fills `company_domain` from the company's WEBSITE (`org.Website`), not from its registered email domains, so this is website-host proof. A member-service lookup of the registered domains and slug replaces it in a later pull request (#6188). Only a website that is a bare host is indexed. A subdomain address (`eng.acme.com`) matches a company that registered the parent; a personal mailbox domain or a public suffix never matches. A company whose email domain differs from its website host is not matched (denied, never admitted), and a website on a shared platform host could match the wrong people.
2. **The company as a present employer in their individual work history.** An entry on the profile's **Work history & Affiliations** tab (`/profile/attributions`) with **no end date** whose employer **name** matches the company's name (`normalizeCompanyName`: case, accents, punctuation, `&`/`and`, a leading "The" and trailing Inc/LLC/Ltd-style words are ignored). The list is read with `CdpService.getWorkExperiencesForKnownUser`, read-only (it never creates a CDP member), and **only the entry's employer name, start date and end date are used**: no CDP organization id, no CDP-to-company mapping, no CDP engagement data.

The company is **found from the validated email** (the active memberships of any foundation, `project_membership`, read with the BFF machine token because the caller was just refused `b2b_org#auditor`, which the query service applies per row; the index of about 10,000 active memberships is read in roughly ten requests, cached per process for 10 minutes, and refreshed in the background (the last good index is served for up to an hour), so only the first load of a process can wait; every membership's status is re-checked) and then **confirmed against the present work-history entries**. The URL never names the company, and a membership carries no slug, so the company is reached by its id: the app links employee-view companies by id, and a shared or bookmarked slug address (`/company/acme-inc/...`) does not open for an employee-view user (a known limit; the id address does). An empty or malformed membership read is a retriable 503, never a cached "not a member".

**One company at a time.** An all employee view user reaches a single company (admins with a grant are unaffected). When several companies meet both rules, the one whose present entry has the **most recent start date** is granted and every other is cut off (`other-company`, 403); a tie resolves to the first in stable order (name, then uid), and two entries naming the same company count once, by the latest start. Nothing is stored: the choice is recomputed from the current work history and emails on each lookup, so changing an employer or a validated email changes the answer at the next check.

**Who is asked at all.** Only a caller with **no Org Lens grant who is not LF staff** (the same entry rule as the CLA-only list) is ever looked up, and the same rule is enforced everywhere: the read gate, `OrgEmployeeViewService.resolveReduced` (the selector row and the address resolver) and role-grants. A grant holder or staff member skips the lookup entirely (`has-grants`), so the selector and the gate agree and admins pay nothing. If the roster cannot be read the answer is a retriable 503, never a denial.

**Not email proof.** The Linux.com forwarding alias (`linux.com` and the stage's `LINUX_FORWARD_DOMAIN`) and consumer ISP mail domains (`ORG_LENS_EMPLOYEE_VIEW_CONSUMER_MAIL_DOMAINS`) never count as a company address, on top of the webmail providers `isPersonalEmailDomain` covers. A strict lookup counts auth-service's primary email only when it equals the email the session verified, and never falls back to the session email.

**Accepted for this release.** A former employee keeps access until they end the work-history entry or the company stops being an active member: the end date is self-edited and verified emails do not expire. This is a known limit, accepted because the view shows aggregates only, with no names, avatars or email addresses, and is closed by the freshness work in (b) below.

**Planned, not in this release.** (a) When the individual profile has a concept of a **primary** employer (written through to CDP), it becomes a third requirement. (b) Once an email validation has a **freshness** concept, the view can expand to show several companies.

The outcome is held 60 s **per caller** (not per requested company) in a cache shared by every `OrgEmployeeViewService` instance, so the role-grants, selector, read-gate and slug-resolver calls of one page share a single lookup. Anything unproven is a denial (403). On `GET /api/orgs/me/role-grants` a lookup that fails or times out is reported as `employeeViewLookup: 'failed'` (never as "not an employee"), and the client shows the retryable outage state. A lookup that **fails** (work history, query service, token, auth-service) is a 503 `ROLE_GRANTS_UNAVAILABLE`, never "not an employee"; a failure is never kept.

Employee access supersedes CLA-only classification for the same company (selector row, address resolver, client state); the caller's own CLA Manager scope is still asked by the EasyCLA gate. The resolver tries the employee view before the CLA scope and, if the employee lookup is unavailable and the CLA scope does not hit, answers a structured 503 `ROLE_GRANTS_UNAVAILABLE` (never a 404 the client would cache). Slug addresses cannot match an employee-view company while the interim lookup has no slug, so they answer not found.

Roll-up segments never qualify: the roll-up gate shares a roster, which skips the employee-view attempt, and drops any company that comes back `employee-view`.

**Rollout flags.** `LFX_ORG_LENS_EMPLOYEE_VIEW_ENABLED` (`ServerFeatureFlag.OrgLensEmployeeView`) is OFF by default and a complete no-op when off. Turn it on only after every page story has applied the tools below; until then an employee-view caller would read pages that are not yet redacted.

**Dev check before turning the flag on.** The company lookup reads the active memberships of any foundation with the BFF machine token (no `b2b_org` grant is needed), and the query service checks each membership row, so the index holds only what that token can read. Confirm in dev: (1) a member-company employee is found and no "Membership read returned no active members" warning is logged; (2) the number of distinct `b2b_org_uid` values in the index matches the expected member-company count, because a partial but non-empty index denies the missing companies' employees and the background refresh serves it for up to an hour; (3) time the first load of a process with the machine token against `ORG_AFFILIATION_LOOKUP_TIMEOUT_MS` (5 s) and `ORG_SLUG_RESOLVE_LOOKUP_TIMEOUT_MS` (3 s): a first load that is slower is a retriable 503 for the callers that waited, while the load completes and a retry succeeds.

**Staff preview.** `LFX_ORG_LENS_EMPLOYEE_VIEW_IMPERSONATION_ONLY` (`ServerFeatureFlag.OrgLensEmployeeViewImpersonationOnly`) opens the view only for a request made under an active impersonation session, so LF staff can impersonate an employee and see what that employee would see in production while no real employee is let in. Real callers are answered exactly as when both flags are off. Every place that opens the view asks `isOrgLensEmployeeViewEnabled(req)` (`org-lens-employee-view-flag.helper.ts`), so the two flags have one definition. Impersonation itself needs the `can_impersonate` claim (see [User Impersonation](./impersonation.md)), so no separate staff check is added. The impersonated user is judged by every rule on this page: no grants, not staff, a validated email on a member company's domain, and that company as a current employer. The staff preview is for pages that have already shipped their redaction; it does not make an unredacted page safe, so keep it off until the page stories you want to check are deployed.

## The mode, for server handlers

```ts
import { getOrgLensViewMode, resolveOrgLensViewMode } from '../helpers/org-lens-read-access.helper';

getOrgLensViewMode(req, orgUid); // 'full' | 'employee', synchronous, memoized per request
await resolveOrgLensViewMode(req, orgUid, operation); // runs the gate first, then answers the mode
```

`getOrgLensViewMode` fails closed: before the gate has resolved on this request it answers `'employee'`. `requireOrgLensAccess` is mounted on `/api/orgs/:orgUid/lens/*`, so the mode is resolved before any lens handler runs. That middleware also answers 403 `EMPLOYEE_VIEW_FORBIDDEN` to any non-GET/HEAD request in the employee view. That read-only guarantee covers the lens prefix only. Writes outside it, such as the company record routes (`PUT /api/orgs/uid/:uid`, `POST /api/orgs/uid/:uid/logo`), are not covered by this gate: they rely on member-service `writer` authorization, and the Profile page story (#5858) additionally blocks them for the employee view (`blockEmployeeViewOnOrgRecord`).

A roll-up segment answers `'full'` from `getOrgLensViewMode` without running any gate. That is a precondition, not an authorization: every roll-up route must stay mounted behind `requireOrgLensAccess` and the roll-up gate, which admit grant holders only. A route that serves a roll-up without them cannot rely on `getOrgLensViewMode` or `blockEmployeeView` to refuse an employee.

## Redaction tools (how a page story uses them)

Each page story applies these to **one** page. Nothing is exposed by default.

1. **Hidden page or side panel: `blockEmployeeView`** (`server/middleware/block-employee-view.middleware.ts`). Mount it on every API route behind it; it answers `403 EMPLOYEE_VIEW_FORBIDDEN` in the employee view and passes everyone else.

   ```ts
   router.get('/:orgUid/lens/people', blockEmployeeView, (req, res, next) => controller.getPeople(req, res, next));
   ```

2. **Shown page with names or emails: `redactForEmployeeView`** (`server/helpers/org-lens-employee-view.helper.ts`). One line in the handler; the full view gets its response untouched.

   ```ts
   res.json(redactForEmployeeView(req, orgUid, result, { page: 'projects', extraFields: ['name'] }));
   ```

   `stripPersonalFields(data, { page, extraFields? })` is the underlying pure helper. It removes the keys in `ORG_LENS_EMPLOYEE_VIEW_PERSONAL_FIELDS` (`email`, `firstName`, `fullName`, `speakerName` ...), the route's `extraFields`, and any value shaped like an email address. String values are also scrubbed of embedded emails, `mailto:` links and personal profile URLs (LinkedIn, X); names in free text are not detected, so a field that carries prose with names needs `blockEmployeeView` or `extraFields`. The default list holds names and email addresses only. An avatar or picture URL, a phone number and a non-public LFID username (`username`, `lfUsername`) are not in it, so a page that returns any of them must pass them through `extraFields`; story #5861 (S16) audits each page for these. A bare `name` is not in the default list (it is also a project or company name), so pass it as `extraFields` where it is a person. Also decide whether a number derived from people is itself revealing.

3. **Exceptions: `ORG_LENS_EMPLOYEE_VIEW_EXCEPTIONS`** (`packages/shared/src/constants/org-lens-view-mode.constants.ts`). Empty at launch. A field on the personal-field list stays visible only through one `{ page, field, reason, approvedBy }` entry decided by the product owner. A key that is not on the list passes without an entry, so an entry would have no effect on it; keep such a key out of a page's payload with `extraFields` or a block, not an exception. A field the route lists in `extraFields` is removed even when an exception names it: the route's explicit removal wins. An exception is keyed by page and normalized field name, so it un-strips that field name everywhere in that page's payload, nested objects included; if the same key also carries personal data elsewhere on the page, handle it with `extraFields` or a handler change rather than an exception.

4. **Hidden page in the client:** add its page segment to `ORG_LENS_EMPLOYEE_VIEW_HIDDEN_PAGES` (same file; the sidebar drops it) and add `canActivate: [orgLensFullViewOnlyGuard()]` to its route, which redirects to Overview in the employee view. The server half is item 1; the UI alone does not count.

## Client

`GET /api/orgs/me/role-grants` carries `employeeView?: { orgUid, orgName, orgSlug? }`, computed only for a caller with no grant and not staff. It is looked up for every grant-less, non-staff caller on a clean roster, with no affiliation pre-filter (that verdict names one employer; this lookup considers all present ones), so LF contractors take the same path, in parallel with the CLA-manager and affiliation lookups. `OrgRoleGrantsService.employeeViewOrgUid` holds it. `OrgLensViewModeService` answers `viewMode()` / `isEmployeeView()` for the selected company (`modeFor(uid)` for others); a real grant or staff always wins. The selector lists the company (`OrgItem.employeeView`), `/api/orgs/resolve/:segment` resolves its address (`OrgResolveResponse.employeeView`), and the empty state opens the lens instead of "no company linked". The client is UX only: the server refuses and redacts regardless.

**Slug addresses.** The employee company is invisible to query-service, so it takes part in slug uniqueness and the `prefer` tie-break alongside a readable company that shares its slug: a shared slug is ambiguous unless `prefer` names one of them. That check is fail-soft (a failed lookup keeps the readable answer). While the interim membership lookup carries no slug (#6188), no employee company has a slug to match, so these checks never find one.

**Client flags are employee-only flags.** `employeeView` on role-grants, the selector row and the resolver is published through `resolveReduced`, which also asks the authorizer for `b2b_org#auditor` on that company. A caller who holds it (an authorizer-only reader such as a key contact absent from the roster) resolves `full` at the read gate, so they get no employee flag and the client keeps the full view. An authorizer failure publishes nothing.

The same overlap needs a positive signal, because the client never stores authorizer entitlements in its grant sets: for such a caller `resolveReduced` returns `holds-full-access` with the company, and the server publishes it as `RoleGrantsResponse.fullAccessOrgUid`, an `OrgItem.fullAccess` selector row (with the company slug) and `OrgResolveResponse.fullAccess`. The client records it (`OrgLensViewModeService.noteFullAccessAddressed`, `fullAccessOrgUids`) so CLA-only classification, addressability, the empty state and the view mode all treat the company as full, even when the caller also manages its CLAs.

## Project detail (story #5849)

The page and its counts and charts stay. **No card panel opens in the all employee view**: the Technical Influence cards (maintainers, contributors, commits, pull requests, average merge time) and every Ecosystem Influence card keep their numbers and charts but open nothing, and a card added later is blocked by default. `blockEmployeeView` guards `/lens/projects/:projectSlug/cards/:cardKey` and `.../cards/:cardKey/roster`, and the paths are also off the allow-list (`ORG_LENS_EMPLOYEE_VIEW_ALLOWED_PATHS`), so the roster and drawer reads answer 403. `blockEmployeeView` also guards `.../leaderboard/ecosystem/organizations/:organizationId`; the technical leaderboard row panel (company scores, no people) still opens. The Projects page's contributors count links to the project detail page without the card parameter, and a `?card=` deep link opens nothing. Every project detail response goes through `redactForEmployeeView`, whose shared strip also replaces an address embedded in a commit message, so no email address leaves the API. The client greys out every card and the ecosystem leaderboard rows (`canOpenCard`, `canOpenLeaderboardDetail`). A future update will rewrite these panels under different access models.

### How this is checked in CI (story #5860)

- `routes/orgs.route.employee-view.spec.ts` reads the route table off the assembled router. Every route behind a page in `ORG_LENS_EMPLOYEE_VIEW_HIDDEN_PAGES` must answer 403 `EMPLOYEE_VIEW_FORBIDDEN` in the employee view and must not in the full view; each blocked panel route and every non-GET lens route is asserted too. A hidden page with no route mapping in that spec fails the suite, so adding a page to the hidden list forces its API to be listed.
- `routes/analytics.route.employee-view.spec.ts` asserts every org-scoped analytics route refuses the employee view, except the four Overview involvement paths in its `ALLOWED_FOR_EMPLOYEE_VIEW` set, which must carry `requireOrgAnalyticsAccessAllowingEmployeeView` (those two checks activate once the Overview story, #5847, is in the base).
- `helpers/org-lens-employee-view-sweep.spec.ts` is the stray name and email sweep: the redaction tool removes every listed field at any depth, and every `org-lens-*.controller.ts` needs a reviewed disposition (`redacts`, `blocked`, `counts-only`, `staff-only`), so a new Company Lens controller cannot ship unreviewed.
- These specs stub the read gate's decision. They do not prove who qualifies as an employee (that is `org-employee-view.service.spec.ts`) and they do not run Playwright: the repo's e2e suite signs in through Auth0 against a real dev server, so the employee-view user journey (allowed pages, nav, redirects) has to be walked by hand against a seeded environment. See the checklist below.

## Rollout checklist for `LFX_ORG_LENS_EMPLOYEE_VIEW_ENABLED`

The flag is OFF by default and a complete no-op when off. Do not turn it on in any shared environment until all of these hold.

1. **Every page story is merged** (#5847-#5859) and `ORG_LENS_EMPLOYEE_VIEW_HIDDEN_PAGES` lists `memberships`, `people`, `easycla`, `profile`, `groups`, `initiatives`, `donations`. The page branches each rewrite that one line, so the merge result must be one array, not seven declarations.
2. **The CI specs above pass** on the merged tree, including the panel and controller-inventory checks that only run once the hidden list is non-empty.
3. **Overview analytics decision made (resolved).** The four Overview involvement routes (`org-foundation-coverage`, `org-involvement-contributors-monthly`, `org-involvement-maintainers-monthly`, `org-involvement-event-attendance-monthly`) return only aggregate counts and series, so the product owner opted them in through `requireOrgAnalyticsAccessAllowingEmployeeView`; their handlers still redact. Every other `/api/analytics/*` row keeps refusing the employee view (`analytics.route.employee-view.spec.ts`).
4. **Exceptions allow-list reviewed.** `ORG_LENS_EMPLOYEE_VIEW_EXCEPTIONS` is empty; confirm that is still the product owner's decision.

## Known open questions

- **Other org-scoped analytics rows** (all except the four Overview involvement rows, e.g. `org-contributors-monthly`, `org-maintainers-*`, `org-training-*`, `org-certified-employees-*`, `org-event-*`, `training-enrollments`, `membership-tier` and similar) are refused for the employee view. That is the safe default; confirm no shown page depends on one.
- **Free text.** `stripPersonalFields` drops email-shaped values and replaces an address embedded in a sentence, in every payload, project detail included, and keeps valid technical text such as a Git remote; meeting titles and event names are not scrubbed.
- **Bare name keys.** `name`, `committerName`, `username`, `donorName`, `supporterName` and `jobTitle` are not in the default personal-field list. Each shown page must pass them as `extraFields` or block the route; the sweep cannot see a payload field a handler adds later.
- **Real data verification.** The specs prove gating and tool behavior with stubbed gates and synthetic payloads. The Snowflake-backed handlers' real responses have not been diffed against the redaction rules for a large account.

## Product decisions (resolved)

- **Small counts are allowed, with no minimum-group-size floor.** A count does not reveal a name.
- **Crowdfunding** initiative detail, transaction and announcement routes (`/api/crowdfunding/initiatives/:slug...`) carry no company id, so the lens gate cannot apply. The pages that reach them (Initiatives, Donations) are hidden in the employee view by the nav and route guards; the company-scoped list, stats and donations reads are refused on the server.
- **Roll-ups** (the all-company roll-up and per-family roll-ups) are not available to employee-view users; they are for admins and staff only.

The review of every personal value an employee-view user could see, and the test that keeps it current, is in [the personal data register](./company-lens-employee-view-personal-data.md).
